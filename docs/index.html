Ranking Content at Risk of Next-Month Search Impression Decline

A public-safe machine learning study of whether March search-performance signals can help prioritize content for human review before substantial impression declines in April.

Author: Azam Hussain · Machine Learning Track · August 2026

Abstract Problem Data Methodology Results Limitations Recommendations Reproducibility Data Credit

Abstract

This study asks whether search-performance information available in March 2026 can rank content items by their risk of a substantial decline in search impressions during April 2026. A public-safe analytical frame of 100,893 eligible content items across 43 anonymized clients was constructed using March Google Search Console features and an April future-outcome label. Logistic Regression was trained using a client-grouped validation design and compared with a transparent rule-based ranking on the same held-out client set using Precision@20 as the primary metric. On the main held-out split, the rule baseline achieved Precision@20 of 0.700 while Logistic Regression achieved 0.800, correctly identifying 16 of the top 20 ranked items as future declines. Stronger five-fold client-grouped validation produced a mean Precision@20 of 0.710, so the result is best interpreted as useful directional decision support for prioritizing human content review rather than as a guaranteed or causal prediction.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams may manage far more pages than they can manually inspect each month. The practical problem is therefore not simply whether a page can be classified as risky, but which pages should enter a limited human-review queue first.

This study evaluates whether information already available at the end of March can provide a useful ranking signal for substantial impression decline during the following month.

Research question: Can March 2026 search-performance information be used to rank content items by their risk of a substantial decline in impressions during April 2026?

The unit of analysis is one anonymized client-content item. The output is a risk ranking, and the supported decision is which items a content strategist should inspect first. The ranking does not automatically edit, publish, prune, or delete content.

Eligibility and exclusions

  • March impressions below 100.
  • Unavailable March GSC data.
  • Unavailable April GSC observations needed for the outcome.
  • Missing or invalid required March performance values.
  • Items that cannot be matched between March and April.
  • GA4 metrics were excluded from the initial feature set because their coverage was lower.

Public-safety check: no client names, private URLs, or raw search queries are included in this paper.

3. Methodology

Target definition

The binary target is declined_next_month. An eligible content item is labeled as a future decline when:

April impressions < 80% of March impressions

This represents a decline of more than 20% between the March decision window and the April outcome window.

Model

Logistic Regression was chosen because the target is binary, the model is relatively interpretable, and its probability estimates can be used directly for ranking.

Validation design

The primary comparison uses an 80/20 grouped train-test split by client_hash_id with random seed 42. Content from the same client is kept entirely on one side of the split.

  • Training rows: 85,702
  • Test rows: 15,191
  • Training clients: 34
  • Test clients: 9
  • Client overlap: 0

A stronger robustness audit also uses five-fold GroupKFold validation by client.

Leakage safeguards

  • April performance is used only to construct the future label.
  • Client and content identifiers are not model inputs.
  • Training and evaluation client groups do not overlap.
  • A separate challenge removes impressions_31d, the feature most directly related to the outcome definition.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Observational, not causal: the model detects associations with future decline and does not prove that any feature causes a search-performance change.
  • Performance varies by client group: five-fold Precision@20 ranged from 0.500 to 0.900.
  • False positives remain: four of the top 20 model-ranked items on the primary holdout did not experience the defined future decline.
  • Limited time horizon: the experiment evaluates one March-to-April 2026 transition.
  • Eligibility-filtered population: the findings apply to content satisfying the study filters and not to every page in the warehouse.
  • Limited feature set: only four GSC-derived features are used.
The model should be interpreted as a directional prioritization tool, not as certainty that a particular page will decline.

6. Ranked Recommendations

1. Review the highest-risk items first. Use the ranked queue to allocate limited human-review capacity to items with the strongest measured decline-risk signal.
2. Investigate low CTR relative to search position. Review title, snippet, intent alignment, and relevance when a page has reasonable search position but unusually weak CTR.
3. Investigate high-visibility content at risk. For higher-impression items, review freshness, relevance, competitive changes, and recent search-performance patterns.
4. Keep uncertain items on a diagnostic watchlist. When the available features do not support a specific explanation, perform a broader manual diagnosis rather than assuming a particular fix.

Freshness is treated as a review hypothesis rather than a guaranteed remedy. Every recommended action requires human review before content is edited, removed, or published.
